How the score is computed

  1. How much it usually moves. Each asset's recent daily swings, weighted towards the last few weeks and blended with its swings over the past year, so one quiet week does not make the range too narrow.
  2. How far 3-day moves really went. Every past 3-day move of every asset in this universe, measured in units of that asset's usual swing at the time. These real moves include the occasional big jump that a textbook bell curve would underestimate.
  3. The 70% range. The middle 70% of those past moves (from the 15th to the 85th percentile), scaled by today's swing and centred on today's price. It does not lean up or down: over 3 days, no one can reliably tell which way a price will go.
  4. Checked, not assumed. When the ranges are fitted, they are tested on recent dates kept out of fitting ("Held when fitted"). Then every live range is checked 3 days later ("Ranges held"). If fewer than about 6 in 10 hold, the method needs revisiting.

Use the range for what it is: how much a price can move, to size a position or decide a stop level. It is not a target. The same method gives the 50% and 90% ranges on the Forecast page. Research tool, not financial advice.
